# Mirror-unsafe ASL signs: which glosses cannot be safely left-right reflected?

Compiled 2026-11-02 for whole-body mirror augmentation on isolated-sign skeletons (WLASL-style). "Mirror" here means reflecting x and swapping left/right joint indices, so a right-dominant signer becomes a left-dominant one.

Evidence labels:
- **VERIFIED**: I read the primary page, paper, code or data file in this pass.
- **VERIFIED-SECONDARY**: the claim comes from a page digest or a secondary paper that describes the primary source.
- **UNVERIFIED**: the claim is recalled or inferred and was not confirmed in this pass.

Source quality: most of the per-sign evidence comes from **practitioner dictionaries** (Lifeprint/Bill Vicars, Handspeak/Jolanta Lapiak, ASL Bloom, Wiktionary). These are not peer-reviewed. The general principle (signs are specified for dominant and non-dominant hand, not for left and right, with lexical direction signs as the exception) is in peer-reviewed work: Shield & Meier 2018, citing Battison 1978. I found **no peer-reviewed study** that lists which individual ASL signs left-handed signers decline to mirror.

## 1. What the literature says

### Takeaway
ASL lexical signs are specified for **dominant and non-dominant hand**, not for left and right. Left-handed signers mirror almost everything. The exceptions with real support are signs whose meaning is an **absolute lateral direction in the signer's space**: LEFT, RIGHT (direction), EAST, WEST, compound compass points, TURN-LEFT/TURN-RIGHT and directional classifier movements. For body-anchored signs on one side of the chest (HEART region, BIRTHDAY ending "over the heart", POLICE badge, LAZY, CHARACTER), the documented left-handed practice is mirroring, or occasionally keeping the absolute side. Either way the meaning does not change.

### Cited findings
- **Shield & Meier (2018), "Learning an Embodied Visual Language: Four Imitation Strategies Available to Sign Learners", *Frontiers in Psychology* 9:811 (peer-reviewed).**
  - The main text, discussing Battison (1978), says "signs are not typically specified for left or right". It adds that children "must learn that signs are not specified for the right or left hand but for dominant and non-dominant." — VERIFIED — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00811/full
  - Footnote 2 says notable exceptions "in many signed languages" are **cardinal-direction signs**: EAST moves rightward and WEST leftward, matching the compass. It adds that "the same is true for the lexical signs RIGHT and LEFT". The footnote credits an anonymous reviewer, so this is a reviewer observation, not a measured result. — VERIFIED (same URL)
  - Lateral-movement signs such as BLACK, SUMMER, BECAUSE, FARM, UGLY, DRY, WE, COMMITTEE, CONGRESS, BOARD, SENATE, ATLANTA, TORONTO and POLAND are described relative to the active hand: they move away from the signer's midline on the side ipsilateral to the active hand. They therefore *mirror* for left-handers. This is a positive list of mirror-safe signs. — VERIFIED (same URL)
  - The deaf signing population is about 90% right-dominant, similar to the general population (citing Conrad 1979, Bonvillian et al. 1982, Sharma 2014, Papadatou-Pastou & Sáfár 2016). — VERIFIED (same URL)
- **Battison (1978), *Lexical Borrowing in ASL*** (Symmetry and Dominance Conditions, framed in dominant/non-dominant terms). — VERIFIED-SECONDARY (through Shield & Meier); I did not read the book.
- **Watkins & Thompson (2017), *Cognition* 164:144–149.** For one-handed signs, all signers responded faster to right-handed input. For two-handed asymmetric signs, left-handers were faster with congruent (left-handed) input. This is a **speed** effect on recognition, not a change of meaning. Mirrored input is still the same sign. — VERIFIED (abstract) — https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-relationship-between-sign-production-and-sign-comprehension-w/
- **Frishberg (dominance reversal), Padden, Brentari, Sandler & Lillo-Martin (2006), Becker.** I did not find any statement in these sources that lists absolute-side exceptions. Sandler & Lillo-Martin (2006) is cited by Shield & Meier, but I did not read it for this question. — UNVERIFIED (not checked in this pass)
- **SignWriting list, Sandy Fleming (2004).** "you can't generate a left handed dictionary automatically except for signs with no 'handedness' location information." Her example is "turn off to the left", which is not a mirror image. With an asymmetric handshape, the left-handed version can be "neither a mirror image nor identical". This is a practitioner list post, not peer-reviewed. — VERIFIED — https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/sw-l/2004-December/005089.html

### Practitioner and dictionary guidance on left-handed signing
- **Lifeprint, "Left-handed signers".** Lefties sign "a mirror image" with the left hand as dominant, and this covers fingerspelling and one-handed signs. "Lefties should also sign letter "J" as a mirror image." The exceptions are "certain signs involving 'directions'": LEFT (L hand moves left), RIGHT (R hand moves right), and a CL:3 "move over to right lane" classifier, where a mirror image "would actually mean, move further to the left". The page adds that rotating or sweeping signs indicate habituality, plurality or verb agreement toward real referents. Its example is "your turn" rotated toward a person on the left. Double letters in fingerspelling slide slightly to the side, and lefties slide left. — VERIFIED — https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-layout/lefthandedsigners.htm
- **Handspeak, "Right- or Left-Handed in Signing?"** Left-handers mirror, "with a few exceptions where you don't use this mirror method when giving a direction". The exception also applies to describing a room or picture from the signer's perspective. — VERIFIED — https://www.handspeak.com/study/52/ and https://www.handspeak.com/topic/5/
- **Handspeak, "Giving and asking for directions".** "whether you're right-handed or left-handed, point to the right side of the signer's torso" (for TURN-RIGHT). Also: "direction is from the signer's perspective." — VERIFIED — https://handspeak.com/learn/193
- **BSL (british-sign.co.uk), for comparison.** Left-handers mirror, but "directional verbs, placement in signing space and role shift – involve more than simply mirroring a video." The page names no individual signs. — VERIFIED — https://www.british-sign.co.uk/left-handed-signing
- **SigningSavvy blog.** Pick a dominant hand and stick with it. Begin and end a fingerspelled word with the same hand. A reader asks whether her Z is "backwards", and the post does not answer. — VERIFIED — https://www.signingsavvy.com/blog/16/Does+it+matter+what+hand+you+use

### Key structural finding: LEFT↔RIGHT and EAST↔WEST relabelling does not produce valid signs
In ASL, LEFT, RIGHT (direction), EAST and WEST are **initialized** signs. ASL-LEX 2.0 codes them with `Initialized.2.0 = 1` and handshapes `l`, `r`, `e`, `w`. A mirrored LEFT is an *L*-hand moving right. That is not the sign RIGHT, which uses an *R*-hand. It is not a well-formed sign at all. Category B (mirror and swap the label) is therefore **not available** for these pairs. — VERIFIED (ASL-LEX signdata.csv; Lifeprint and Handspeak handshape descriptions)

B relabelling only fits **non-initialized** direction pairs that share a handshape, such as classifier "turn left" and "turn right". No such pair is a separate gloss in WLASL-2000.

## 4. Categories and coding criteria

### Definitions
- **A: never mirror (absolute-side meaning).** The gloss denotes a lateral direction or side in the signer's space. Left-handers keep the direction, so a mirrored sample is a wrong or ill-formed sign.
- **A?: exclude by caution.** There is no ASL evidence that mirroring changes the meaning, but there is a precedent in another language or a single weak source, and excluding the gloss is cheap.
- **B: mirror and relabel to the opposite gloss.** Only valid when the opposite gloss uses the same handshape and differs only in lateral direction. **Empty for WLASL-2000.** The LEFT/RIGHT and EAST/WEST pairs fail because the signs are initialized with different handshapes.
- **C: whole-body mirror is fine.** Every other sign, provided the augmenter swaps left/right joint labels and keeps facial landmarks consistent.

### Coding rules for a coder (ASL-LEX 2.0 fields)
ASL-LEX 2.0 has **no laterality field**. `MajorLocation` takes the values Head/Arm/Body/Hand/Neutral/Other. `MinorLocation` values such as TorsoTop, TorsoMid, Clavicle and Shoulder do not say ipsilateral or contralateral, and the phonological-coding supplement never mentions ipsilateral, contralateral or left/right. — VERIFIED (signdata.csv and Supplementary_Materials_Phonological_Coding.docx, https://osf.io/zpha4/). Body side cannot be read off ASL-LEX, so the rules below are semantic first:

1. **Semantic rule (decisive).** Is the gloss's meaning an absolute lateral direction or side (left, right, east, west, any compass point with an E or W component, turn-left or turn-right, "to the left/right side")? Then **A**.
2. **ASL-LEX corroboration for rule 1.** These signs typically have `MajorLocation.2.0 = Neutral`, `SignType.2.0 = OneHanded`, `Movement.2.0 = Straight` and `Initialized.2.0 = 1`, with handshape the initial letter (l, r, e, w). Rule 1 plus `Initialized = 1` means B relabelling is invalid; use A.
3. **Anatomical-organ rule.** The gloss names a lateralised organ (heart, liver, appendix, spleen, left/right kidney) and the sign touches the torso (`MajorLocation = Body`, `MinorLocation` ∈ {TorsoTop, TorsoMid, TorsoBottom}). Use **A?** and decide by cost. For HEART and HEART ATTACK in WLASL, ASL-LEX codes the citation form as near-midline (symmetric tracing or a middle-of-chest tap), so the risk is low.
4. **Hand-label rule.** A gloss about handedness itself (RIGHT-HANDED, LEFT-HANDED; RSL "правша") is **A**: its form likely references a specific hand. Neither is in WLASL-2000.
5. **Everything else is C.** This includes `MajorLocation = Arm` (TIME, DOCTOR, NURSE, HOSPITAL: the non-dominant arm, so it mirrors), torso-side signs whose side is defined relative to the dominant hand (BIRTHDAY, LAZY, CHARACTER, POLICE), pronoun pointing, citation-form agreement verbs, numbers and fingerspelling.
6. **Mixed-sense glosses.** WLASL merges senses under one gloss. If any sense is A, either exclude the whole gloss or exclude it per instance by source URL (see "right" below).

## 6. Gaps and caveats
- No peer-reviewed per-sign inventory of non-mirrored ASL signs exists that I could find. The list rests on dictionary and practitioner pages plus one peer-reviewed footnote that came from a reviewer.
- HEART is the main open question. Asking a Deaf left-handed signer, or checking the left-handed WLASL clips of `heart`, would settle it cheaply.
- ASL-LEX's ~2,700 signs cover about 1,360 WLASL glosses by name match (approximate). It cannot flag laterality, so the rules above are semantic.
- Some WLASL signers may already be left-handed. For A glosses, their clips still move in the correct absolute direction, so a flip produces the wrong direction whatever the source handedness.
- The −1.3% WLASL flip result (Kvanchiani) is RGB-based and not attributed to specific glosses. It does not show that the handful of A glosses caused the drop.
- I did not check: Frishberg on dominance reversal, Sandler & Lillo-Martin (2006) chapters on the non-dominant hand, ASL Signbank gloss pages (they may need a login; the search form shows handedness values such as "Weak Drop" but no laterality field), and SigningSavvy video entries (member-gated text).
