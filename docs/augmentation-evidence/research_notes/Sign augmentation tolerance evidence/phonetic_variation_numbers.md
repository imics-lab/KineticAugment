# Quantitative phonetic variation in sign production that preserves sign identity

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: numbers (mm, degrees, ms, % of tokens, normalized units) for how much a sign varies across signers, repetitions, rates, registers and contexts while staying the same sign. Language is ASL unless flagged **[NGT]** (Sign Language of the Netherlands) or another language.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = I read the primary full text or abstract at the URL. **VERIFIED-SECONDARY** = read in a source that cites it. **EXISTS** = bibliographic record only. **UNVERIFIED** = search-engine summary only.

Headline: the claim "no source gives numeric tolerances in mm or degrees" needs revising for **location**. Three Optotrak studies (Tyrone & Mauk 2010, 2012; Mauk & Tyrone 2012) publish per-signer means, SDs and shifts in mm. They are descriptive spreads from small samples (4–6 signers), not perceptual thresholds. For **handshape**, **palm orientation** and **movement amplitude**, I found no published within-category spreads in degrees. The glove studies give methods, correlations or qualitative plots instead.

## Location: how far signs drift across tokens, rates, contexts and signers

### Takeaway
Within one signer, one forehead sign and one condition, the token-to-token SD of the hand's vertical peak is about **11–59 mm** (Optotrak, 6 signers, ASL WONDER). Faster rate or a low neighbouring sign moves the location by **10–43 mm vertically, 15–140 mm laterally and 5–119 mm front-back**. Mean location for the same sign differs between signers by more than **100 mm** in a head-centred frame. In a video corpus, forehead signs show long downward tails, while chin and mouth signs stay tightly clustered. Lowering is one-directional (downward) and stops at the eye.

### Cited findings

**Tyrone & Mauk (2010), *J. Phonetics* 38:317–328. ASL WONDER (forehead sign), 6 native Deaf signers, Optotrak, 60 Hz, 10 tokens per speed per condition, 120 productions per signer.** VERIFIED (full text) — [PMC2895670](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2895670/)
- Reference frame: head-centred, with the **chin as zero** on all three axes. The marker is on the dorsal hand at the third-metacarpal midpoint, not the fingertip. The measure is the vertical maximum of the sign. Negative values mean the hand marker is below the chin.
- Table 1, per-signer vertical position, mean (SD) in mm, pooled over 3 rates:
  - DP: 73.4 (20.3), 84.1 (11.1), 73.0 (19.4), 81.9 (18.1)
  - DSM: −4.2 (51.7), −2.0 (59.3), 7.4 (35.8), 34.8 (32.9)
  - EB: 64.3 (12.7), 68.3 (13.2), 64.0 (14.4), 66.9 (10.8)
  - JP: −5.3 (47.1), −23.4 (41.4), −0.4 (46.0), −5.6 (36.8)
  - KM: 1.9 (13.6), 5.3 (22.9), −1.4 (23.0), −6.1 (22.6)
  - RD: 114.2 (21.7), 96.8 (32.0), 107.2 (18.6), 100.3 (25.3)
  - Order within each signer: BITTER-initial, BITTER-final, ME-initial, ME-final.
- **Between-signer spread of mean location for the same sign is about 137 mm** (JP-final −23.4 mm to RD-initial 114.2 mm). That is my calculation from Table 1.
- **Within-condition SD is 10.8–59.3 mm.** Footnote 4 says these SDs include variation from both context and rate.
- Table 1, sign duration, mean (SD) in ms: 603 (213) to 1086 (562) across signers and contexts.
- Location-on-duration regression slopes are almost all positive: longer (slower) tokens are higher. The slopes are **+1.6 to +21.6 mm per extra 100 ms of duration**, with R² from 0.00002 to 0.89. Only one slope is negative (DP BITTER-final, ≈0).
- Context and position effects reported in the text:
  - KM is about 7 mm lower in the ME context, which the authors call "quite small".
  - DSM is about 37 mm higher in the ME context, utterance-final.
  - DSM utterance-final is about 28 mm higher than utterance-initial.
  - RD utterance-initial is about 12 mm higher than utterance-final.
- The paper reports no velocities, no per-rate means and no forehead-relative values.

**Tyrone & Mauk (2012), *Laboratory Phonology* 3(2):425–453. 4 native signers; KNOW, WHY, FATHER, STUBBORN; 3 rates; about 60 productions per sign per signer; Optotrak Certus, 60 Hz, 6 Hz Butterworth low-pass.** VERIFIED (full text) — [PMC4489421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4489421/)
- Reference frame: head-centred. The origin is the lowest right marker of a 6-marker head device. The hand marker is ulnar-side. Location is taken at the positional extremum that coincides with a velocity minimum.
- Table 1, phonetic-environment effect at normal rate (low neighbour minus high neighbour):
  - Vertical: 10–40 mm lower in 7 of 14 comparisons. Examples: KNOW VF 40 lower; STUBBORN VF 34 lower; FATHER LL 23 lower; STUBBORN RD 20 lower.
  - In 2 comparisons the hand was 10 mm *higher* (FATHER MF, FATHER RD).
  - Lateral: 15–88 mm (STUBBORN VF 88 right; FATHER RD 53 left; KNOW VF 42 right).
  - Front-back: 5–13 mm.
- Tables 2–5, rate effect: predicted position at the longest versus shortest phrase duration.
  - Vertical: 2.2–42.9 mm (FATHER LL high-environment 42.9 down; STUBBORN LL low-environment 42.5 down; KNOW RD high-environment 41.7 down).
  - Some signers move **up** with rate (KNOW LL high 24.7 up; STUBBORN LL high 25.7 up).
  - Lateral: up to 139.7 mm (FATHER RD high) and 75.2 mm (WHY MF high). Rate shifts were consistently leftward, toward the midline for right-handers: "No significant rightward shifts occurred as an effect of rate."
  - Front-back: up to 118.8 mm (WHY RD low, forward) and 78.4 mm (FATHER RD low, back).
- The authors argue that shifting forward or sideways "cannot create a different lexical item."
- The paper reports no percentage of tokens lowered and no absolute durations.

**Mauk & Tyrone (2012), *Sign Language & Linguistics* 15(1):128–146. Optotrak 60 Hz plus Vicon (30 markers, 100 Hz).** VERIFIED (full text) — [PMC4606891](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606891/)
- Lateral extent of signing space, one signer (VF): **275 mm at normal rate versus 285 mm at fast rate**.
- At fast rate, the top of the vertical range rose by about 30 mm, and the low/left endpoint dropped by about **150 mm**.
- Vertical location of KNOW correlates with the endpoint of the *following* sign (r² = 0.78) much more than the preceding one (r² = 0.14), independent of rate.
- Hand-to-sternum distance is reported only as a ratio of each signer's maximum anatomical reach, with no mm values.

**Russell, Wilkinson & Janzen (2011), *Laboratory Phonology* 2. Conversational video of 6 right-handed Deaf native signers (Winnipeg); 3,075 tokens; 229 sign types at the head or neck; NTSC 30 fps, 640×480.** VERIFIED (author draft, full text extracted) — [author draft PDF](https://home.cc.umanitoba.ca/~krussll/papers/Russell,%20Wilkinson,%20and%20Janzen%202011%20draft.pdf)
- Units are **pixels**, measured from the canonical passive articulator at (0,0). The frame is rotated by the inter-eye angle, and left-handed tokens are reflected. Eyes, nose and chin were marked for "rough normalization", but results are not reported in normalized units.
- Lexical category (mixed model): verbs are displaced an extra **−7.9 px** relative to nouns (t = −3.14). Nouns' mean displacement of **−4.8 px** is not different from 0.
- Lexical frequency: **−4.5 px per unit** of subjective frequency on a 7-point scale (t = −2.54). It is −4.4 px when location region and lexical category are controlled.
- The eye "forbidden zone" sits about **30 px below the forehead passive articulator**. This gives an in-paper scale: forehead target to eye centre ≈ 30 px.
- Plot axes for the attainment-point scatter run from about −200 to +50 px vertically and −80 to +40 px horizontally. BROTHER, FATHER and MAN show downward tails; SISTER, MOTHER and WOMAN stay tight.
- Lowering is large at the forehead, smaller at the eyes, and small or arbitrary at the nose, mouth, chin and neck (Fig. 5).
- KNOW is bimodal: one forehead cluster and one cheek cluster. The corpus has 170 KNOW tokens and 72 DON'T-KNOW tokens.

  - Tunnel-vision signers: **97.3%** in conversation and **90.7%** in monologue.

  - Blind versus sighted was not significant (p = .846).
  - The proportion of signing near the face therefore varies by about ±15–25 percentage points with visual status and genre.
- Emmorey, Gertsberg, Korpics & Wright (2009), *Applied Psycholinguistics* 30:187–203: Optotrak, 5 conditions (blindfolded, tunnel vision, citation, shouting, informal).   - Tunnel vision reduced vertical movement. Blindfolded and citation signing differed only in duration.
  - The mm values were not retrieved because the UCI PDF returned 404.
- Crasborn (2001) [NGT]: shouting enlarges and whispering reduces movement, as already covered in the prior review. I found no mm values in accessible text.

### Inferences
- Treat **±10–20 mm (1 SD) of 3-D hand-location jitter** as within normal token-to-token variation for one signer at citation-like rates. The pooled SDs in Tyrone & Mauk 2010 are 11–59 mm, and they include rate and context effects, so a pure same-condition SD is probably at the low end.
- Rate and coarticulation shifts of **20–40 mm vertical** and **up to 50+ mm lateral or front-back** are attested for forehead signs without a change in identity. Displacements above 100 mm appear for single signers in single conditions.
- Lateral rate shifts go **toward the midline**, so they are asymmetric, not zero-mean.
- Between-signer differences in the mean head-relative hand position exceed 100 mm for the same sign. Much of this is probably anatomy (hand size, marker placement) plus style. A per-clip global offset for each signer is therefore realistic, but it must be applied in a face-anchored frame and must not cross a lower-location minimal pair (BROTHER/SISTER).
- Converting the Russell pixel values with the in-paper forehead-to-eye ≈ 30 px scale, and assuming a forehead-to-eye distance of about 3–4 cm (my assumption, not from the paper), gives roughly 1–1.3 mm per px. On that rough scale, the noun mean of −4.8 px is about −0.5 cm and the verb extra of −7.9 px is about −1 cm. Tails reach 10–20 cm below forehead targets.
- The asymmetry matters more than the magnitude. Lowering goes downward only, it is large at the forehead and small at lower locations, and it never enters the eye. Lower targets (chin, mouth) cluster tightly in video, so their tolerance should be smaller.

### Gaps
- No study gives **perceptual** location thresholds (the displacement at which signers re-identify the sign) in mm. Categorical-perception work found no CP for place of articulation and reports no mm boundaries.
- Mauk (2003) dissertation, "Undershoot in two modalities" (UT Austin): EXISTS — [hdl 2152/762](https://hdl.handle.net/2152/762). Its numbers were not reached; the repository abstract gives no values.
- The Mauk et al. (2008) undershoot paper is cited by Tyrone & Mauk, but I could not locate its content or confirm its author list.
- Ormel, Crasborn & van der Kooij (2013), *J. Phonetics* [NGT], "Coarticulation of hand height in NGT is affected by contact type": 5 signers, CyberGlove plus Flock of Birds. EXISTS / VERIFIED-SECONDARY (abstract via search) — [record](https://cnlse.es/es/recursos/biblioteca/coarticulation-hand-height-sign-language-netherlands-affected-contact-type). Its mm values were not retrieved. The strongest coarticulation was at the weak-hand location and was modulated by contact type. Bibliographic records conflict on the volume (41 vs 51).
- I found no between-signer *normalized* signing-space size (for example, as a percentage of arm length) for ASL.

## Movement: amplitude, velocity and duration across repetitions, signers and registers

### Takeaway
Duration varies widely and is the main thing that changes with rate. ASL WONDER ran 603–1086 ms per signer, with within-signer SDs of 180–590 ms. ASL-LEX citation signs average 851 ± 301 ms. Fingerspelled letter-to-letter transitions run 224–498 ms per signer. Rate changes also move location by tens of mm, as in the previous section. I found no per-sign SDs of path amplitude or peak velocity in accessible full text.

### Cited findings
- WONDER duration per signer and context, mean (SD) in ms:
  - DP: 928 (316) and 866 (452)
  - DSM: 1054 (587) and 1086 (562)
  - EB: 1027 (291) and 1082 (315)
  - JP: 722 (180) and 768 (269)
  - KM: 851 (329) and 871 (246)
  - RD: 688 (268) and 603 (213)
  - Source: Tyrone & Mauk 2010, Table 1, pooled over 3 elicited rates. VERIFIED — [PMC2895670](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2895670/)
- **Mean duration differs by up to about 1.8× between signers** for the same sign and context (RD-ME 603 ms vs DSM-ME 1086 ms). The coefficient of variation within a signer is about 25–55%. My calculation from the table above.
- Hold-to-hold transition times in fluent fingerspelling: grand means 300–327 ms for words and 348–351 ms for non-words. Per-subject values range from 224 ms to 434–498 ms, which is about 3–4 letters per second. Coarticulatory influence spans about 1.5 letters (about 0.5 s). Source: Jerde, Soechting & Flanders (2003), *J. Neurosci.* 23(6):2383–2393, 4 interpreter signers, CyberGlove, Table 2. VERIFIED — [PMC6742053](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6742053/)
- Rate is controlled mainly through sign duration, not the number of signs (Wilbur 2009). Telic verbs show greater endpoint deceleration (Malaia & Wilbur). Both are in the prior review. Their numeric mm/s values were not retrieved: the Malaia lab PDF returned 404.
- Imitation error rates, as a proxy for how often a movement-direction or orientation feature is misproduced. Shield & Meier (2018), *Front. Psychol.* 9:811 (Study 1: non-signers N = 34, ASL learners N = 25, fluent signers N = 18). VERIFIED (full text) — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00811/full)
  - Overall errors: 6.85% for non-signers, 2.63% for learners, 1.39% for fluent signers.
  - Lateral-movement errors: 24.3%, 14.3% and 6.3%.
  - In–out palm-orientation errors: 4.04%, 0.17% and 0.23%.
  - Up–down palm-orientation errors: 5.04%, 0.83% and 1.16%.
- L2 learners: Hilger, Loucks, Quinto-Pozos & Dye (2015), *Second Language Research*. They measured wrist-displacement stability with the spatiotemporal index (STI) on 8 signs in carrier phrases. EXISTS / VERIFIED-SECONDARY (abstract via search) — [record](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1177/0267658315570648). STI values were not retrieved.
- Jantunen (2013), *Sign Language Studies* [FinSL]: velocity and acceleration of signs versus transitions, from 120 Hz, 8-camera optical mocap. EXISTS (abstract via search) — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/75961?show=full). Numbers were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Time-warp ranges of about 0.6–1.8× the median duration** fall inside attested inter-signer and inter-rate spread for a lexical sign. Uniform speed-ups shrink amplitude and shift location toward neighbours or the midline, so pure temporal resampling without a spatial change is less realistic at the fast end.
- Fluent signers rarely misproduce movement direction or palm orientation: about 1% of imitations overall and 0.2–1.2% for orientation. Flips of movement direction or orientation are therefore not natural variation.

### Gaps
- I found no accessible numbers for peak velocity (mm/s) or path amplitude (mm) per sign across repetitions or signers, and none for whisper versus shout or child versus adult. Emmorey et al. (2009, Applied Psycholinguistics) and Crasborn (2001) are the likely sources but were not reached.
- Child proximalization rates: Meier, Mauk, Cheek & Moreland (2008, *Language Learning and Development*) EXISTS. Mirus, Rathmann & Meier (2001) EXISTS. No percentages were retrieved.

## Handshape: joint-angle variability within a category

### Takeaway
No accessible study reports within-category SDs of finger flexion or spread in degrees for native signers. The glove studies (Jerde et al. 2003; Eccarius, Bour & Scheidt 2012) report methods, correlations and qualitative clusters. Eccarius et al. state outright that the amount of acceptable phonetic variation between handshapes is unknown. The best quantitative anchors are glove resolution and calibration accuracy, a few example joint angles, and evidence that a handful of joint angles suffice to separate letters.

### Cited findings
- Jerde, Soechting & Flanders (2003). VERIFIED — [PMC6742053](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6742053/)
  - CyberGlove with 17 DOF: MCP and PIP of all five digits, 4 abductions, thumb rotation, wrist pitch and yaw. Angular resolution under 0.5°, sampled every 12 ms.
  - Their earlier work classified static letters correctly 88% of the time using only 4 joint angles.
  - Coarticulation was reported only as correlations. Little-finger MCP r = +0.65 to +0.86; index MCP r ≈ −0.50 (critical r = ±0.39).
  - Mahalanobis distances between letters were computed, but no values are reported.
- Eccarius, Bour & Scheidt (2012), "Dataglove measurement of joint angles in sign language handshapes", *Sign Language & Linguistics* 15(1):39–72. VERIFIED (full text) — [PMC3758436](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3758436/)
  - Calibration: variance accounted for (VAF) of 98.9 ± 1.7% for MCP and 99.3 ± 0.89% for PIP flexion. Abduction VAF falls to about 92.6% at 60° middle–ring spread.
  - Example angles from one pilot signer, describing a shirt (not means):
    - The index MCP ranges from about −20° (hyperextended, in a "large-dot" classifier) to about 60° across successive handshapes.
    - The middle-finger PIP is about 50° in that classifier.
  - Fingerspelled E: one subject's index MCP was "slightly flexed" and the other's "slightly hyper-extended". So the **same letter category spans flexion versus hyperextension at the MCP across two signers**. This is qualitative; no degrees are given.
  - O handshapes plotted with 95% confidence ellipses, without numbers.
  - Classifier continua: only 2–3 handshape clusters appeared where 5–6 sizes were described.
  - Summarizing Eccarius (2008): round versus flat O classifiers contrast only at the extremes, and the midpoint is ungrammatical as a shape classifier. All three O variants are acceptable for core and initialized lexical signs.
  - The paper says "there is a great deal of acceptable phonetic variation" between handshape phonemes, but that thresholds are unknown.
- Cheek (2001, dissertation, UT Austin): measured pinky extension as the fingertip-to-wrist marker distance in 1 and 5 handshapes flanked by 1 or 5 handshapes. Variation was rate-dependent: a 1 handshape preceded by a 5 handshape had a more extended pinky. Interpreted as coarticulation, not assimilation. VERIFIED-SECONDARY, via Eccarius et al. 2012 and search summaries — [PMC3758436](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3758436/). No numbers were found in citing sources.
- Keane, Brentari & Riggle (2015), "Segmentation and pinky extension in ASL fingerspelling": less coarticulatory pinky extension when the pinky is selected and flexed (A, S, E, O) than when it is non-selected. VERIFIED-SECONDARY (abstract via search) — [talk abstract](https://voices.uchicago.edu/lvc/?p=427). No degrees or proportions were retrieved. Keane (2014, UChicago PhD), which models handshape from joint angles, EXISTS.
- de Meijer, Ormel, Kootstra & Crasborn (2017), *Laboratory Phonology* 8(1), doi:10.5334/labphon.45 [NGT]. VERIFIED-SECONDARY (abstract via search) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/68451f72ec534227bd6f584f15fb7006)
  - Corpus NGT; **728 tokens, 14 B-handshape sign types, 61 signers**. Thumb state (2 dimensions) and finger spread were coded by hand from video.
  - The thumb state of the preceding and following signs significantly predicts the target's thumb state, and so does finger spread.
  - So in the B handshape, the thumb is a free, coarticulated dimension. Percentages were not retrieved; the journal site was bot-blocked.
- Bayley, Lucas & Rose (2002), 1-handshape variation: more than 5,000 tokens from more than 200 signers (prior review). The variant percentages were not retrieved from accessible text.

### Inferences
- Glove studies agree that non-selected fingers (the pinky in 1 handshapes, the thumb in B) vary freely and coarticulate. Selected, flexed fingers resist that pull. This supports **larger jitter on non-selected fingers and the thumb in open handshapes, and smaller jitter on selected fingers**, without a degree figure.
- Two pilot signers producing the same fingerspelled E at opposite sides of 0° index MCP suggests within-category spans of at least **±10–20° at some joints**. This is an inference from qualitative text, not a measured SD.

### Gaps
- No within-category SD in degrees was found for any handshape. No category boundary was found in degrees: the Emmorey et al. (2003) and Baker et al. (2005) categorical-perception continua are image morphs and were not reported in joint angles.
- The full texts of Keane (2014) and Cheek (2001) were not reachable. They are the most likely sources of degree or mm values for pinky extension.
- Ann (2006), on ease of articulation, gives frequency and anatomical-difficulty scores rather than variance. It was not checked.

## Palm orientation: measured variability

### Takeaway
I found no study reporting palm-orientation variability in degrees for native production. The only numbers are error rates in imitation: fluent signers reverse in–out or up–down orientation in only about 0.2–1.2% of imitations. Orientation can trade off with location and handshape in enlarged or reduced registers (Crasborn 2001).

### Cited findings
- Fluent signers' palm-orientation errors in imitation: 0.23% (in–out) and 1.16% (up–down), against 4.04% and 5.04% for non-signers. Shield & Meier (2018). VERIFIED — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00811/full)
- The CUNY ASL motion-capture corpus records palm orientation, handshape (CyberGloves) and location, but its publications report no per-sign variance statistics (see the corpora section).

### Inferences
- Without published numbers, tie the orientation tolerance to the spreads in the location section (rate shifts toward the midline imply some forearm rotation) and set it empirically from data.

### Gaps
- No degree-valued orientation variability was found in ASL, NGT or LSF sources.

## Motion-capture sign corpora with per-sign variance statistics

### Takeaway
The large ASL mocap corpora (CUNY) were built for animation and report no per-sign spread. The usable per-sign variance tables come from the small Optotrak lab studies by Tyrone and Mauk (location section).

### Cited findings
- Lu & Huenerfauth, "Collecting and evaluating the CUNY ASL Corpus for research on ASL animation" (preprint, about 2013): 8 male signers aged 21–34 (mean 27.9); 242 annotated passages over 11 sessions (212 min). The released portion is 98 passages from 3 signers, averaging 97.7 signs per passage. The equipment was CyberGloves, body suit and head/eye tracking. VERIFIED (full text extracted)
  - **The preprint contains no per-sign variance statistics** for location, handshape or orientation (grep of the full text). Its research focus is the placement of spatial reference points and verb paths.
  - Sources: [preprint PDF](https://huenerfauth.ist.rit.edu/pubs/lu-huenerfauth-2013-csl-preprint.pdf); access by request via the [lab downloads page](https://latlab.ist.rit.edu/downloads.html).
- Jantunen et al. [FinSL] run an 8-camera, 120 Hz optical mocap pipeline. EXISTS — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/75961?show=full)
- LSF mocap by Gibet and colleagues, and DGS MOCAP1: not reached in this pass. I found no per-sign variance numbers.

### Inferences
- The CUNY corpus, if access is granted, could be mined directly for per-sign location and glove-angle spreads across 8 signers. That would be the only ASL source with multi-signer, data-glove handshape variance.

### Gaps
- Per-sign SDs from the CUNY, LSF (Gibet) and MOCAP1 (DGS) corpora are unknown. Mauk (2003) and the Emmorey et al. (2009) kinematic paper were not obtained in full text.
